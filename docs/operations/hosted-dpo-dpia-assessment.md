## A. Must a DPO be designated?

Answer **yes / no / uncertain**, explain facts and cite evidence for each row. Test controller
and processor activities; the provider cannot merely rely on customers' assessments.
Bases: GDPR Art. 37(1), Art. 38 and §38 BDSG, sources A1–A3 below.

| Criterion | Answer / evidence / reasoning |
| --- | --- |
| Public authority/body under Art. 37(1)(a), excluding courts acting judicially | [Assess status; do not assume merely from public-service availability] |
| Core activities require regular and systematic monitoring of people on a large scale, Art. 37(1)(b) | [Describe whether monitoring is core, regular/systematic and large scale; routine IT support alone is not automatically the trigger] |
| Core activities involve large-scale Art. 9 special categories or Art. 10 criminal data, Art. 37(1)(c) | [Assess uploads/free text and real intended processing; incidental possibility alone does not establish large scale] |
| Generally at least 20 persons constantly engaged in automated personal-data processing, §38(1) sentence 1 BDSG | [Count people by actual tasks, including relevant part-time personnel; record method, not just full-time equivalents or developer count] |
| Processing subject to a DPIA under Art. 35, §38(1) sentence 2 BDSG | [Use section B; this trigger is independent of headcount and also addresses processors] |
| Businesslike processing for transmission, anonymized transmission, or market/opinion research, §38(1) sentence 2 | [Assess actual purpose; ordinary instructed hosting is not automatically data brokerage] |

## B. Is a DPIA required for each processing activity?

The controller assesses before likely high-risk processing. The processor supplies information
and assistance under Art. 28(3)(f); it does not make customers' determinations for them. Assess
fogu's own controller activities separately and record relevant customer DPIA requirements for
its processor activity and the national DPO trigger (A1/A2).

| Test | Answer / facts / applicable list item / evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Art. 35(3)(a): systematic/extensive automated evaluation, including profiling, supporting decisions with legal or similarly significant effects | [Include any scoring/selection affecting tenants; distinguish assistance from consequential automated decisions] |
| Art. 35(3)(b): large-scale Art. 9/10 processing | [Categories, volume, population, duration and geographic extent] |
| Art. 35(3)(c): systematic large-scale monitoring of publicly accessible areas | [Assess actual scope; ordinary hosting is not automatically such monitoring] |
| Art. 35(4): German authorities' mandatory list | [Check every potentially matching DSK v1.1 item and the competent authority's current list; record exact item/conditions and reasons for match or non-match] |
| Art. 35(1): likely high risk even without a listed example | [Reasoned assessment using the criteria below; absence from a list is not an exemption] |

Apply the nine WP248/DSK risk indicators (A3/A4), with a yes/no and reasons for each:

- Evaluation/scoring, including profiling and predicting.
- Automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effects.
- Systematic monitoring.
- Sensitive or highly personal data (including financial information).
- Large-scale processing: people/proportion, volume/range, duration and geographic extent.
- Matching/combining datasets.
- Vulnerable subjects, including children or people with unequal bargaining power.
- Innovative technological/organizational use, assessing actual effects rather than the label “AI”.
- Preventing exercise of rights or use of a service/contract.

Two indicators normally point toward a DPIA under WP248; one can suffice. This is guidance,
not a mechanical statutory two-point threshold or a safe harbour below it. Explain any departure
and consider DPO advice if appointed. Neither a free service nor encryption alone establishes
low risk. Consider confidentiality, integrity and availability harms to people (A1/A3/A4).
